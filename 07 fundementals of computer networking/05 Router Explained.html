🌍 How a Router Works: Complete Knowledge

1. What is a Router?

A router is a networking device that:

  • Connects two or more networks (e.g., your home LAN ↔ the ISP’s WAN/Internet).
  • Decides where packets should go (forwarding).
  • Performs translation and management between private and public networks.
  • It works at Layer 3 (Network Layer) of the OSI model.

2. The Two Faces of a Router

A home router typically has at least two "sides":

LAN side (Local Area Network)

  • Uses private IP addresses (192.168.x.x, 10.x.x.x, 172.16.x.x).
  • Distributes IPs to devices using DHCP.
  • Acts like a switch/bridge inside the home.

WAN side (Wide Area Network)

  • Connected to your ISP.
  • Uses a public IP (sometimes shared if behind CGNAT).
  • Provides access to the internet.

Routers actually always have at least two IP addresses

1. WAN (Public-side) IP

This is the IP your ISP assigns to your router.
It connects your router to the internet.
Example: 39.43.29.189 (public IPv4).
Sometimes this is a real public IP → directly reachable from the internet.
Sometimes it’s a private/shared IP (CGNAT) → not directly reachable.

2. LAN (Private-side) IP

This is the IP your router uses to talk to your local devices (laptop, mobile, smart TV).
Example: 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.10.1.
Your devices get their IPs from this router via DHCP.

3. Key Functions of a Router

  • Routing: Maintains a routing table to direct packets to the correct network.
  • NAT (Network Address Translation): Replaces private source IPs with the router’s public IP, enabling multiple devices to share one public IP.
  • DHCP (Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol): Acts as a server to automatically assign network configurations (IP, subnet mask, gateway, DNS) to devices.
  • DNS Forwarding: Forwards DNS resolution requests from devices to an ISP or public DNS server.
  • Firewall & Security: Blocks unsolicited inbound connections to protect the LAN.
  • Switching & Bridging (Layer 2): Includes an Ethernet switch for wired connections and acts as a wireless access point for Wi-Fi devices.

4. Packet Flow Example

🔄 Laptop → Google (HTTP request)

  1. Laptop sends packet to its default gateway (192.168.1.1).
  2. Router performs NAT, replacing laptop's private IP with its public WAN IP (39.43.29.189).
  3. Router sends the packet to Google.
  4. Google replies to the public IP.
  5. Router checks its NAT table and forwards the reply back to the laptop's private IP (192.168.1.10). ✅

5. Advanced Router Features

  • Port Forwarding: Directs specific external traffic to a device on the LAN.
  • QoS (Quality of Service): Prioritizes certain types of traffic (e.g., gaming, video).
  • VPN: Encrypts traffic between you and a VPN provider.
  • Dual Stack: Supports both IPv4 and IPv6.
  • CGNAT (Carrier Grade NAT): ISP-level NAT for shared public IPv4 addresses.

6. IPv4 vs IPv6 in Routers

  • IPv4: Requires NAT because private IPs are not routable on the internet.
  • IPv6: NAT is often not required as every device can have a globally unique IP address.

7. Summary Analogy

Think of your router as the reception desk of an apartment building:

  • Residents = your devices (private IPs).
  • Street Address = your public IP.
  • Receptionist:
    • Assigns room numbers (DHCP).
    • Keeps a visitor log (NAT table).
    • Forwards mail (routing).
    • Blocks unwanted visitors (firewall).

✅ A router is a multi-role device: gateway, translator, switch, firewall, and manager.
