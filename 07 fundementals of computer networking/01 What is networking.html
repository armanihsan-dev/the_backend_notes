Understanding "Network"

The term "network" comes from the ideas of "net" and "work." "Net" refers to an interconnected system, like a web, while "work" refers to the function or operation of that system. The combination of these ideas gives us "network," an interconnected system that "works" to accomplish a task.

What is a Network?

A **network** is simply a group of two or more things that are interconnected and can exchange information or resources. It's a broad term used in many fields, from social networks of people to electrical power grids. In computing, a network is a system of interconnected computers and other devices, like printers or servers.

What is Networking?

Networking is the process or action of creating, managing, and using a network. For example, a person engages in social networking when they interact with others to build professional connections. In the context of computers, networking is the act of configuring and maintaining the hardware and software that allows devices to communicate with each other.

What is Computer Networking?

Computer networking is the specific field and practice of connecting computing devices to share data and resources. This involves a set of rules, known as **protocols**, that dictate how the devices communicate. These protocols ensure that data is transmitted correctly and securely. The Internet itself is the largest and most well-known example of a computer network.
