🔐 Authentication vs. Authorization 2

While often used together, **authentication** and **authorization** are two distinct, sequential processes essential for securing an application.

Feature Authentication (AuthN) Authorization (AuthZ)
Question it Answers "Who are you?" (Verifying Identity) "What are you allowed to do?" (Verifying Permissions)
Process Proving one's identity to the system. Determining if an authenticated user has the right to access a resource or perform an action.
Common Method Usernames and passwords, biometric scans, security tokens, or multi-factor authentication (MFA). Access control lists (ACLs), role-based access control (RBAC), or attribute-based access control (ABAC).
When it Happens First. The user must be authenticated before they can be authorized. Second. Happens after successful authentication.

🔑 Authentication 3

**Authentication** is the process of verifying a user's claimed identity. It confirms that the person trying to access the application is who they say they are.4

Purpose and Need

  • **Identity Verification:** It establishes a trustworthy link between a user and the system.5
  • **Accountability:** By tying actions to a verified identity, the system can track who did what.
  • **Prerequisite for Authorization:** No resource can be protected without first knowing who is asking for it.6

Importance for an App

Without authentication, anyone could claim to be anyone else, leading to **impersonation** and **uncontrolled access** to basic features or public data.

🛡️ Authorization 7

**Authorization** is the process of granting or denying an authenticated user access to specific resources, functions, or data within the application.

Purpose and Need

  • **Principle of Least Privilege (PoLP):** It ensures users only have the bare minimum access necessary to perform their job, minimizing the potential damage from errors or malicious intent.8
  • **Data Segmentation:** It keeps different users' or groups' data separate (e.g., preventing one user from seeing another user's private account details).
  • **Role Enforcement:** It allows an application to define different **user roles** (like 'Administrator', 'Editor', 'Guest') and automatically associate different permissions with them.9

Importance for an App

Even after proving their identity (**authentication**), a user shouldn't be allowed to do everything. Without authorization, a regular customer could:

  • Access the admin dashboard.
  • View or modify other customers' private data.
  • Change the application's core settings.
Authorization is critical for **data integrity**, **privacy**, and **system stability**.10

🌟 Overall Importance for an Application

Authentication and authorization are crucial because they fulfill the core requirements of application security:

  • **Security and Privacy:** They are the primary defense against unauthorized access to sensitive data (like payment information, personal records, or proprietary business data), preventing data breaches and protecting user privacy.11
  • **Compliance:** Many industry and government regulations (like **GDPR**, **HIPAA**, **PCI DSS**) mandate strict controls over who can access and manage personal or financial data.12 Proper AuthN/AuthZ is necessary to meet these legal requirements.
  • **Trust and Reputation:** Users will not use an application they don't trust to keep their data safe.13 Robust security builds user confidence and protects the company's brand reputation.14
  • **System Integrity:** By controlling write access, they prevent unauthorized users from corrupting or destroying data, ensuring the application remains functional and reliable.
