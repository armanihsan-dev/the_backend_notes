How Binary Data is Represented on a Physical Level in Computers

At its core, all digital data within a computer (in the CPU, RAM, etc.) is represented by electrical signals or voltage levels.

  • Logic 1 (ON): Represented by a high voltage (e.g., 3.3V or 5V, depending on the system's design).
  • Logic 0 (OFF): Represented by a low voltage (typically 0V or close to it).

These voltage differences are detected and processed by billions of tiny transistors within integrated circuits (chips). A transistor acts as a switch, allowing current to flow (ON/1) or blocking it (OFF/0), forming the fundamental building blocks of logic gates and memory cells.

How Data is Represented on Storage Devices

Hard Disk Drives (HDDs)

HDDs use magnetic storage to represent binary data.

  • Structure: HDDs consist of rapidly spinning platters coated with a magnetic material. Read/write heads float just above the platter surfaces.
  • Representation:
    • To write a '1' or '0', the read/write head's electromagnet creates a tiny magnetic field that polarizes (magnetizes) a microscopic area on the platter in one of two directions. One direction represents '1', and the opposite direction represents '0'.
    • To read data, the head detects the magnetic orientation of these areas, which induces a tiny electrical current that is then interpreted as a '1' or '0'.
Physical Change: A physical change in magnetic polarity on the disk surface.

Solid State Drives (SSDs)

SSDs use NAND flash memory to store data, relying on electrical charges.

  • Structure: SSDs are composed of NAND flash memory chips, which are essentially grids of memory cells. Each cell contains one or more floating-gate transistors (FGTs).
  • Representation:
    • FGTs have the ability to trap and store an electrical charge. The presence or absence of a charge (or different levels of charge) in the floating gate determines whether a cell represents a '0' or a '1'.
    • For example:
      • Single-Level Cell (SLC): Stores one bit per cell. A charge might represent '1', and no charge might represent '0'.
      • Multi-Level Cell (MLC), Triple-Level Cell (TLC), Quad-Level Cell (QLC): Store multiple bits per cell by precisely controlling and detecting different levels of charge within the floating gate. For instance, four distinct charge levels could represent 00, 01, 10, and 11 (2 bits).
Physical Change: A physical change in the electrical charge stored within the floating gate of the transistor.

How Data is Represented During Transfer

Wi-Fi (Wireless)

Wi-Fi transmits data using radio waves (electromagnetic waves).

  • Representation: Binary data (0s and 1s) is converted into radio signals through a process called modulation.
    • Different Wi-Fi standards (e.g., 802.11n, 802.11ac, 802.11ax) use various modulation techniques. Common methods include:
      • Amplitude Shift Keying (ASK): Varying the amplitude (strength) of the radio wave.
      • Frequency Shift Keying (FSK): Varying the frequency of the radio wave.
      • Phase Shift Keying (PSK): Varying the phase of the radio wave.
      • Quadrature Amplitude Modulation (QAM): A combination of amplitude and phase modulation to encode multiple bits per signal change, increasing data rates.
    • A '1' might be represented by one specific change in amplitude/frequency/phase, and a '0' by another.
Physical Change: Changes in the properties (amplitude, frequency, phase) of the electromagnetic waves in the air.

Fiber Optics

Fiber optics transmit data using pulses of light.

  • Structure: Fiber optic cables contain thin strands of ultra-pure glass or plastic (the core), surrounded by a cladding layer.
  • Representation:
    • Binary data is converted into light pulses by a light source (like a laser or LED) at one end of the cable.
    • A light pulse (ON) represents a '1'.
    • The absence of a light pulse (OFF) represents a '0'.
    • The light travels down the fiber by undergoing total internal reflection, bouncing off the cladding, until it reaches a receiver at the other end that converts the light pulses back into electrical signals.
Physical Change: Presence or absence of photons (light energy).

Copper Wire (Ethernet, USB, etc.)

Copper wires transmit data using electrical signals (voltage).

  • Representation: Similar to how binary is represented inside a computer's CPU and RAM, data is sent as varying voltage levels.
    • A high voltage (e.g., +5V or +3.3V) can represent a '1'.
    • A low voltage (e.g., 0V or -5V) can represent a '0'.
    • In many modern network cables (like Ethernet), more complex signaling techniques like differential signaling (using the voltage difference between two wires) and multi-level signaling (using more than two voltage levels to encode multiple bits per signal change) are employed to achieve higher data rates and reduce noise.
Physical Change: Flow of electrons (current) and potential difference (voltage) in the metal wire.

In essence, while computers process information as abstract 0s and 1s, at the physical level, these bits are manifested as tangible differences in physical properties: magnetic orientation, electrical charge, light pulses, or voltage levels.
